PMID: 41668558
Title: Characteristics and prognosis of Epstein Barr virus-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma: a retrospective, single-center study.

Abstract:
To analyze the clinical characteristics and prognosis of Epstein-Barr virus-positive diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (EBV<sup>+</sup>DLBCL) in a Chinese cohort.
Fifty-seven EBV<sup>+</sup>DLBCL patients (diagnosed between 2013 and 2020) and 228 EBV-negative DLBCL (EBV<sup>-</sup>DLBCL) controls were included. Differences were compared and prognostic factors were identified using univariate and multivariate analysis.
EBV<sup>+</sup>DLBCL patients (median age, 56 years; 38 men [66.7%]) predominantly had non-GCB origin (70.2%). Compared with EBV<sup>-</sup>DLBCL, EBV<sup>+</sup>DLBCL patients more frequently presented with B symptoms, hypoalbuminemia, anemia, >1 extranodal site involvement, and elevated LDH andβ2-microglobulin (all <i>P</i> < 0.05). The common extranodal sites were the digestive tract (stomach), nasopharynx, bone marrow, and bone. CD30 positivity was higher in EBV<sup>+</sup>DLBCL patients (42.1% vs. controls, <i>P</i> < 0.001). After R-CHOP-like therapy, EBV<sup>+</sup>DLBCL patients achieved complete and overall response rates of 55.5% (30/54) and 83.3% (45/54), respectively. With a median follow-up period of 28 months, the relapse rate was 22.2% (10/45). The response and relapse rates were similar between groups. Multivariate analysis identified elevatedβ2-microglobulin and bone marrow invasion as independent risk factors for worse progression-free survival (PFS), and elevatedβ2-microglobulin plus decreased hemoglobin for worse overall survival (OS) in EBV<sup>+</sup>DLBCL.
EBV<sup>+</sup> DLBCL in this Chinese cohort was primarily non-GCB. Elevatedβ2-microglobulin and bone marrow invasion independently predicted inferior PFS, while elevatedβ2-microglobulin and anemia predicted inferior OS.
