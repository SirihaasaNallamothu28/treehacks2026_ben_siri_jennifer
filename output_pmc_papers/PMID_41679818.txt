PMID: 41679818
Title: Structural characterization and hypoglycemic mechanisms of a Hericium coralloides heteropolysaccharide (HCP-2b) via gut microbiota modulation and PI3K/Akt signaling.
Journal: Carbohydrate polymers
Authors: Wang, Mengyu; Fu, Hao; Lv, Guoying; Zhao, Ruimeng; Cai, Weiming; Pi, Xionge; Zhang, Zuofa

Abstract:
This study isolated, purified, and characterized a novel polysaccharide, HCP-2b, from the fruiting bodies of Hericium coralloides, and investigated its hypoglycemic potential and underlying mechanisms. Bioactivity-guided fractionation yielded HCP-2b, which was structurally characterized as an amorphous heteropolysaccharide (9.93 kDa) with a backbone comprising →2,6)-α-D-Galp-(1→, →6)-β-D-Glcp-(1→, →6)-α-D-Galp-(1→, →3)-β-D-Glcp- (1→ and →3,6)-β-D-Glcp-(1→. In vitro assays demonstrated that HCP-2b significantly inhibited α-glucosidase and α-amylase activity and delayed glucose diffusion. Moreover, HCP-2b enhanced glucose uptake, attenuated oxidative stress, and suppressed mitochondrial permeability transition pore opening in insulin-resistant (IR) HepG2 cells. Furthermore, in a T2DM zebrafish model, HCP-2b effectively ameliorated hyperglycemia. In addition, HCP-2b modulated gut microbiota composition, selectively enriching Bacteroides and Parabacteroides while increasing propionic acid production in vitro fecal fermentation. Western blotting analysis revealed that HCP-2b upregulated the expression of key proteins (Akt, P-Akt, IRS-1, PI3K, GLUT4) in the PI3K/Akt pathway. The hypoglycemic effect of HCP-2b was significantly attenuated by treatment with PI3K and Akt inhibitors in zebrafish model, confirming the involvement of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway. These findings demonstrated that HCP-2b might exert antidiabetic effects through a potential mechanism with gut microbiota modulation, metabolite production, and activation of the PI3K/Akt signaling pathway, positioning it as a promising multifunctional agent for T2DM management.
