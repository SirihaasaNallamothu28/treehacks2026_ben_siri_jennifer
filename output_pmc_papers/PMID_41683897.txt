PMID: 41683897
Title: A Hydrolase-Rich Venom Beyond Neurotoxins: Integrative Functional Proteomic and Immunoreactivity Analyses Reveal Novel Peptides in the Amazonian Scorpion <i>Brotheas amazonicus</i>.
Journal: International journal of molecular sciences
Authors: Wiezel, Gisele Adriano; Bordon, Karla de Castro Figueiredo; Martins, Jonas Gama; Custódio, Viviane Imaculada do Carmo; Matsuno, Alessandra Kimie; Procópio, Rudi Emerson de Lima; Arantes, Eliane Candiani

Abstract:
The scorpion family Buthidae, renowned for its neurotoxin-rich venoms, dominates toxinology, while non-buthid venoms remain largely unexplored. Here, we present a comprehensive proteomic and biochemical characterization of the Amazonian chactid scorpion <i>Brotheas amazonicus</i> venom (BamazV), with emphasis on molecular complexity, proteolytic processing, and peptide diversity. Using an integrative venomics approach that combines molecular mass-based fractionation, reversed-phase chromatography, high-resolution mass spectrometry, N-terminal sequencing, and functional and immunological analyses, we reveal an unexpectedly complex venom profile enriched in high-molecular-weight components and extensively processed peptides, with more than 40 venom peptides sequenced by MS/MS and Edman degradation. The data provide evidence for non-canonical proteolytic events, including the generation of peptides from precursor regions not classically associated with mature venom components. In contrast to the venom of <i>Tityus serrulatus</i>, BamazV displays a "hydrolase-rich, neurotoxin-poor" profile, featuring a catalytically active Group III phospholipase A<sub>2</sub> (BamazPLA<sub>2</sub>), a highly active hyaluronidase, metalloproteases, low-mass peptides, and potassium channel toxins. Our results suggest a hydrolytic prey-subjugation strategy, and limited cross-reactivity with commercial antivenom highlighted its distinct structural landscape. Overall, this study advances the understanding of venom evolution and proteolytic diversification in underexplored scorpion lineages, positioning <i>B. amazonicus</i> as a valuable model for investigating alternative venom strategies and identifying novel biotechnological scaffolds.
