PMID: 41688200
Title: [Analysis of five-year outcomes and risk factors for insufficient weight loss after laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy in obese patients].
Journal: Zhonghua wei chang wai ke za zhi = Chinese journal of gastrointestinal surgery
Authors: Li, T C; Wang, W J; Wang, J; Ni, L C; Hong, J; Zhu, X C; Yao, L B

Abstract:
<b>Objective:</b> To explore the five-year outcomes of obese patients after undergoing laparoscopic sleeve gastrectomy (LSG), and to identify risk factors for insufficient weight loss. <b>Methods:</b> A retrospective observational study was conducted. Inclusion criteria: (1) obese [body mass index (BMI)>28 kg/m<sup>2</sup>] patients receiving LSG; (2) complete clinical data with 5-year follow-up; (3) no history of gastrointestinal surgery. Clinical data of 173 patients who underwent LSG from May 2014 to June 2019 at the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University were collected. Primary outcomes included changes in body weight, BMI, %TWL and %EWL; insufficient weight loss (%TWL<50% at 5 years) was recorded and its influencing factors were analyzed. Secondary outcomes included remission and new onset of comorbidities. <b>Results:</b> A total of 173 obese patients who underwent LSG surgery with complete follow-up data were included in the study, including 76 males and 97 females, with a mean age of (33.6±9.8) years. The patients' preoperative, 1-year postoperative, 3-year postoperative and 5-year postoperative BMIs were (40.2±7.0) , (27.4±4.5) , (28.4±4.5) and (29.4±4.7) kg/m<sup>2</sup>, respectively. The patients' 1, 3, and 5-year postoperative %TWL were (31.0±8.1) %, (28.3±8.4) % and (26.3±8.4) %, respectively, and the 1, 3, and 5-year postoperative %EWL were (85.4±27.9) %, (78.2±28.0) % and (71.4±26.7) %, respectively. The effect of postoperative weight loss was different in patients with different levels of obesity. Compared with severe obesity, patients with super-obesity had higher %TWL [(33.0±6.7)% vs (28.9±8.0)%] but lower %EWL [(59.0±13.6)% vs (65.6±18.9)%] at 5 years. Logistic regression analysis showed that preoperative high BMI (OR=1.208, 95%<i>CI</i>:1.086-1.343, <i>P</i><0.001) was the independent risk factor for insufficient weight loss in patients. At 5 years after surgery, the rates of complete remission of hypertension and diabetes were 43.2% (19/44) and 67.2% (39/58), respectively. The remission rate of gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD) was 8/16, while the new onset rate was 31.4% (37/118). <b>Conclusion:</b> LSG has a good long-term therapeutic effect on weight loss, type 2 diabetes and hypertension, but there is an increased risk of postoperative GERD, and patients with greater preoperative weight are more likely to suffer from inadequate weight loss in the postoperative period.
